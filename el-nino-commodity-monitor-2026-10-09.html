Change from 28 September

The climate event strengthened. The broad commodity trade did not.

NOAA’s 8 October update materially increases confidence in an exceptional event: El Niño probability is 100% through JFM 2027, strong-to-very-strong conditions through JFM are above 83%, and the chance of a historic-strength three-month RONI peaks at 83% in OND. Indonesia is already unusually dry and its wet-season onset is delayed. But the balance evidence still points to selective, lagged risk rather than a broad shortage: U.S. old-crop corn stocks are 35% above a year earlier, South Africa’s commercial maize estimate is a record 17.523 Mt, and AMIS still calls global grain supply prospects generally favourable.

Current stance: unchanged—watch and wait for physical confirmation; do not initiate a broad El Niño commodity basket solely from the forecast. Raise monitoring priority for lagged palm and robusta risk, but do not force the available arabica or broad-basket proxies. Reduce conviction in corn, broad grains and near-term Southern African maize scarcity.

Why: the climate leg is stronger, but the crop-loss, market-balance and instrument-fit legs are not. This is a research stance, not a personal trade or allocation recommendation.

Regions and crop calendars

Where the forecast meets sensitive windows

Indonesia / Malaysia · palm oil

BMKG reports broad late-September deficits, delayed wet-season onset and low rainfall across 66.09% of Indonesia for 11–20 October. Current stress matters mainly through lagged bunch formation and 2027 yields. Malaysia’s latest output and stocks still require an observed downturn before the balance view changes.

Vietnam / Indonesia · robusta

Vietnam’s main harvest begins from October; dry weather can initially aid picking, while prolonged stress threatens later flowering. Current robusta exports and certified stocks still resist a realized-deficit conclusion.

India / Thailand · sugar

India’s monsoon tail overlaps cane growth and sucrose accumulation; Thailand approaches crushing. Reservoirs, cane weight, recovery rates, area and ethanol policy—not rainfall maps alone—must confirm the physical effect.

South Africa · white/yellow maize

October–December planting and establishment are now the most immediate forecast-to-crop overlap. The record 17.523 Mt commercial crop is the opening buffer. White maize is regionally specific and is not cleanly represented by global corn futures.

Ghana / Côte d’Ivoire · cocoa

The October–March main crop is beginning. Rainfall, pod counts, disease and port arrivals matter, but ICCO still withholds the current-season production and grinding estimates needed for a balance conclusion.

Global corn / wheat / rice

U.S. corn harvest is four points behind the five-year pace and condition fell three points, but old-crop stocks are substantially above a year earlier. AMIS still sees generally favourable supply, with wheat and rice offsetting its lower maize-production forecast.

Decision gates

Strengthen, weaken, invalidate

Strengthen

Repeated crop-region deficits persist into bunch formation, flowering or planting; MPOB output/stocks turn down; Vietnam robusta arrivals or exports tighten; official cane recovery falls; WASDE cuts maize availability beyond the stock offset; West African cocoa arrivals disappoint; and a usable instrument matches the confirmed physical channel.

Weaken

Rain normalises before sensitive stages; palm output/stocks stay ample; Vietnam robusta arrivals remain strong; South African planting establishes normally; cane estimates hold; U.S./global grain balances improve; cocoa arrivals recover; or prices move without corresponding physical evidence.

Invalidate

An exceptionally strong El Niño persists without adverse anomalies in the target crop regions; official balances improve despite weather; observed moves are explained by policy, FX, energy, freight or positioning; or the available product tracks a different contract than the physical thesis.
